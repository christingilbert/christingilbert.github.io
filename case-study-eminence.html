The Situation

A campaign photography shoot ran roughly $7,000 once photography, post-production, coordination and asset prep were counted. Shorter campaign cycles and more digital channels meant more shoots needed, not fewer — with quality and cost both expected to hold steady.

Stakeholder Needs

Most of the creative team came from print, packaging and traditional graphic design. Interest in generative AI was already there — individual designers had started experimenting on their own — but nothing connected that experimentation to a production-ready asset. Three groups needed three different things from the same process.

Stakeholder Needs
The Approach

I built and ran a three-session internal series, Snack & Learn: AI for Image and Video Generation, moving the team from curiosity to a repeatable workflow over fall and winter 2025.

01
Text to Image
Prompt structure fundamentals and tool orientation across ChatGPT, Midjourney, Magnific AI and Adobe Firefly.
02
Still to Motion
Text-to-video and image-to-video workflows, and a structured, JSON-style prompt format.
03
Content to Campaign
Campaign-level workflow: master prompt development, content guardrails and derivative-asset architecture across channels.
Governance & Guardrails

Guardrails did two jobs at once: they kept AI-generated visuals inside brand guidelines, and they told leadership exactly where AI was, and wasn't, in use.

AI is approved for
  • Backgrounds & environments
  • Props and secondary elements
  • Mood and lighting exploration
  • Concept and moodboard generation
  • Campaign visual testing
AI is not approved for
  • Product efficacy claims
  • Skin transformations or results
  • Product textures
  • Before/after visuals
  • Realistic human results tied to products

Tool-access rules sit on top of that: which shared accounts were in use, how prompts were logged, and who owned them — full transparency from brief to final asset, especially for anything entering paid media.

Prompt Architecture

The same campaign brief now produces a locked master prompt, a set of flexible variants, and derivative assets split by still and motion — each one routed to the channel it's built for.

Prompt Architecture

The result: 15–20 on-brand still images and 2 motion assets per campaign, generated from a documented, repeatable process rather than a new negotiation every time.

Chapter 02 · Visual System

A Shared Reference for What "On-Brand" Means

The Gap

Brand guidelines set identity: values, voice, colour. They didn't say how a hero banner resizes for mobile, what alt text a product photo needs, or which layout block fits a small product page. Designers made different calls on the same questions, and cross-functional teams worked from different assumptions about what "on-brand" meant in practice.

I developed the Digital Visual Style Guide as a living document — leading the structure and visual system, and working with the project manager and website team on technical specification. It translates brand guidelines into rules a team can apply directly to a layout.

Four Principles

Four principles give the team a shared vocabulary for visual mood and tone in day-to-day decisions.

Principle 01
Sensory Experience
Tactile textures and spa-like environments carry the visual mood.
Principle 02
Simplicity & Minimalism
Clean compositions and generous negative space keep the product as the visual focus.
Principle 03
Organic & Natural Elements
Wood, stone, leaves and fabric reinforce the brand's connection to organic ingredients.
Principle 04
Emotion-Driven Imagery
Personal-care moments, with representation across skin tone and gender expression.
Structure

Product Detail Pages break into content blocks, each with a fixed content type, aspect ratio and accessibility requirement. Built as a modular library in Figma, the blocks combine into small, medium, large and extra-large configurations without a new layout decision every time. The same logic extends to Product Listing Pages, with defined grid, image-ratio and hierarchy rules for browsing and navigation.

From the Digital Visual Style Guide: PDP block structure, content types and aspect ratios.

Accessibility Standards

Accessibility is built into the system itself, not reviewed in afterward — every content module carries its own rules for contrast, alt text and placement.

  • 01 · Maintain sufficient colour contrast A contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, UI controls and graphical objects — WCAG AA minimum across every digital touchpoint, with exceptions only for decorative and disabled elements.

What Changed

Teams reference one document instead of re-aligning on layout and accessibility at the start of every campaign. The standard doesn't change depending on who — or what — produced the layer underneath: it's the same reference the AI guardrails in chapter one exist to enforce.

Chapter 03 · Production & Asset Management

A Framework Built While the Campaign Shipped

The Situation

Website, landing pages, social, email, paid ads, editorial: six channels, each needing assets shaped to its own spec and signed off before it ships. Spring 2026 was the test of whether a production framework could survive contact with an actual deadline, not just live on a slide.

A Five-Stage Framework

Five stages, repeatable for any campaign — performance data, creative direction, pre-production, production and asset delivery — each one feeding what the next campaign starts from.

Spring 2026

The Spring 2026 Advanced Care Collection campaign, Transitional Aftercare, carried one visual thread through two shoot environments — a professional spa setting and an at-home aftercare scene — tied together by consistent wardrobe, palette and props. A model shortlist went to senior leadership for approval before the shoot; finished assets went through brand and accessibility review before distribution across all six channels.

Homepage hero banner, Spring 2026 Advanced Care Collection — live on eminenceorganics.com.

Asset Management

The framework needed somewhere to put what it produced. Earlier in the same six years, I redesigned the DAM taxonomy and restructured the asset library — standardized metadata, consistent naming — so that finding an existing photo took less time than re-shooting one that already existed, unfound.

6,000+ Assets restructured under the new taxonomy
15 Multi-stakeholder photo & video shoots managed

Managing 15 multi-stakeholder shoots over the same period meant the taxonomy had to hold up under real production pressure, not just in a slide — fewer duplicate searches, less re-shooting of work the library already had.

Outcomes

The framework built for Spring 2026 is the one the next campaign starts from — the same decisions, reused, not remade.

 

The guardrails, the visual standards and the asset library aren't three separate projects. They're the same kind of decision, made once, so the team doesn't make it again every campaign.
