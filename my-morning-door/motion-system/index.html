Motion system

How the door moves.

My Morning Door is a multi-sensory Chrome new tab for a calmer start, and its motion carries the calm: a circle that breathes with you, a door that opens and closes at its own pace, words that update in place, and sound that fades in and out and starts only when asked. This page records every duration, easing and rule in the shipped code, with demos built from the same constants. Nothing here plays until you press it.

  • Layers CSS, Web Animations, canvas, audio
  • Slowest the door closing, 2000 ms
  • Quickest a chip on hover, 200 ms
  • Modes Full, Gentle, Still
  • Frame budget about 30 fps, 1.5x pixels

Reading this from the portfolio? The principles and the Living Threshold demo carry the thinking. Everything after that is the reference: timings by layer, beat-by-beat choreography, motion modes and performance rules.

Why it moves like this

Eight principles

Each was learned from a screen recording that felt wrong. They are ordered from the one that decides most to the one that decides least.

1

One mark at a time

Unlike marks take turns. A crossfade of the door and the rings would read as a double exposure, so the outgoing mark leaves first, then the incoming one arrives. One mark on screen at any moment.

Rule: unlike drawings take turns; like drawings morph as one.

2

One object, changing

The circle is one thing at different sizes. During a transition both scenes are painted at a single blended scale, and the anchor glides between layouts.

Rule: during a morph both layers share one scale, so the eye sees one circle.

3

Painted time

A click can block the main thread for hundreds of milliseconds. A wall-clock fade is then already mostly over when the next frame lands, so it pops. Fades advance only by frames that actually paint, capped at 70 ms per frame.

Rule: a stall pauses a fade.

4

Even-paced easings

When a frame drops mid-transition, a fast-start curve loses its whole opening and reads as a cut. The dissolves spread their change evenly, so a dropped frame loses a sliver.

Rule: even-paced curves on everything that fades.

5

Motion follows content

Transitions are keyed to what changed on screen. Choosing a rhythm changes only the copy, so only the copy dissolves. Only the resting threshold, which has a different layout, earns a whole-screen crossfade.

Rule: in the flow, only the panel's contents fade; the panel stays.

6

Speed says what kind of moment it is

Hover 220 ms. Copy dissolve 420 to 560. Ring morph 620. Different drawings 900. The door opening 1250. The door closing 2000, because the wind-down is the moment worth lingering on.

Rule: durations only go up as the moment matters more; keep the ladder.

7

Idle means breath

Everything at rest moves the way breathing does: slowly and slightly. The resting mark breathes at 0.8 alpha ± 0.035 over 15 seconds. The rings drift ± 1.8 %. Two points orbit slowly. It should feel alive.

Rule: idle amplitude under 2 %, periods over 10 seconds.

8

The person sets the level

Full, Gentle and Still are choices in Preferences. Still and the system's reduced-motion setting render one static frame and stop the loop. An explicit in-app choice wins over the system default, both ways.

Rule: respect the OS by default; obey the person when they choose.

Tempo

Speed hierarchy

Every duration in the product, shortest first. The rule that keeps them honest: a duration only grows as the moment matters more. Hover is a flicker of attention. The door closing is the last thing you see before you go back to work.

Below 1000 ms are the transitions; the bars are to scale. Above are the rhythms people breathe and rest to, listed, and the 4 hour session gate that decides whether a tab shows the full pause.

Curves

Easings

Every fade in the product runs on an even-paced curve, so a dropped frame costs a sliver rather than the opening. The one spring is reserved for icon parts on hover. The full list of durations by layer sits in the technical reference.

Choreography

Every transition, beat by beat

Two things move on every click: the canvas (one drawing changing) and the DOM (the words). They run on different clocks, side by side; each is designed to look right alone and to agree in tempo.

The door opens

Resting to arrival. The whole screen crossfades in the DOM (the greeting cluster out, the panel in), while the canvas plays a two-beat sequence: the door mark fades where it stands, then the rings bloom from the door's own circle edge. During the exit beat the anchor glide is held, so the mark does not slide or grow while it leaves.

leavingarrivingheld or waiting

The door closes

Any flow screen back to resting: the wind-down. Slower on purpose. It was slowed twice in testing before the ending felt unhurried. The rings gather back to 0.62 and fade over the first 40 %, then the mark settles in over the remaining 1200 ms.

Ring morph and family fade

Between two ring scenes (arrive → breath-ready → phases → complete) the transition is a 620 ms morph: both scenes are painted at one blended scale, so the eye sees one circle changing size. Between a ring scene and a grounding or release drawing it is a 900 ms crossfade of two different drawings; those are different enough that overlap does not read as a double exposure.

In-flow click: the copy morph

Every click inside the flow (arrival → practice → steps → complete). The glass panel stays put. Before the DOM is replaced, the panel's content is cloned into a ghost; after the replacement, in the same frame, the ghost fades out over the live panel while the new content fades in and rises 9 px, and the panel height eases. The clone carries the hovered control's paint so the button under the cursor keeps its paint.

Good afternoon.

A short breathing, grounding, or muscle-relaxation practice is here if you need it.

  • Capture before replacing the DOM: clone the panel's children, strip ids and the title marker, copy the hovered control's paint, keep any child's current opacity if it was still fading in.
  • Ghost is a positioned wrapper carrying the old screen's layout class, inert and aria-hidden, with the panel chrome removed so only one blur exists.
  • Out 420 ms cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.3, 1), fill forwards, then the ghost is removed.
  • In each new child: opacity 0 → 1 and translateY 9 px → 0, 560 ms after 40 ms, cubic-bezier(0.45, 0, 0.25, 1), fill backwards.
  • Height old → new, 520 ms cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1), only when it differs by more than 1 px.
  • Settled the screen gets .screen-settled first, so the CSS enter fade stays off.

Breathing

The rings own the rhythm. Each phase is dispatched to the canvas at the exact boundary, so every phase keeps its full length. The words dissolve on their own quieter clock: title out 680 and note out 620 (60 later), then title in 860 and note in 900 (90 later). Roughly 1.6 seconds of the phase is a soft change of words; the circle keeps its own time.

Guided steps

Grounding and muscle release can run by themselves or wait for a press. In automatic pace grounding advances every 15 s and release every 10 s; the final step always waits. Each advance is a normal render, so the copy morph is the dissolve; there is no second fade on top (an earlier build stacked one and stuttered).

Hover and focus

Small, and only on things you can press. The doorway row gains white 0.075 and slides 6 px right in 220 ms; its icon parts move on a 700 ms spring: wind lines drift 1.5, 3 and 2 px, sun rays turn 8° and grow 4 % while the core grows 8 %, the seated figure settles 1 px. Rhythm options lift 2 px. Arrows move 3 px. Phase dots grow to 1.35 over 500 ms. Focus states are static: outline plus under-ring, no animation.

Live: hover the rows

Reveal and idle

  • First paint: the canvas starts 250 ms after the page and fades in over 900 ms. The open feels instant; the threshold arrives.
  • Atmosphere: the two soft radial overlays change opacity per scene over 1.4 s (breath 0.62, ground 0.76, release 0.52, complete 0.42, resting 0.38).
  • Resting mark: sin(t·0.42), a 15 s cycle. Scale ± 1.2 %, y ± 0.8 % of r, alpha 0.8 ± 0.035. Reads as full white; the movement is barely visible and mostly felt.
  • Rings at rest: sin(t·0.46) × 0.018 (Gentle 0.008), points orbiting at 0.18 and −0.13 rad/s.
  • Holds in Box Breathing: ± 0.006 of sin(π·t), a living stillness.

Sound

Audio has motion too, and the same rules. Everything starts with a press. Sound comes in over 900 ms. Beds loop with an equal-power crossfade of 1.5 to 3 s so the seam stays inaudible. When a voice clip plays, the bed ducks to 42 % over 260 ms and restores afterwards while the loop keeps running underneath. Stop fades in 700 ms; a timed session ends with a 2 s fade. Opening more tabs leaves the sound exactly as it was: one instance, one volume.

Choice

Motion modes

Three levels, chosen in Preferences, stored locally. The system's reduced-motion setting is only the default. Once someone has chosen in the app, that choice wins in both directions: a person may choose Full on a machine that prefers reduced motion, and Still on one that does not.

The demo on this page follows the same rule: if your system prefers reduced motion, it starts in Still, and you may still choose Full.

The calm budget

Performance rules

Smoothness is part of calm. A stutter reads as the product being nervous. These rules keep an expensive glass blur and a living canvas on screen together at full frame rate, on a 2× display, in a browser tab.

One backdrop-filter at a time

Two stacked blurs were what made the old transition heavy and flashy. Clones carry the copy only, and header pills use paper.

Clear only the damage

Each frame clears only the union of the last and current drawing box (1.35 r + 40 px). Idle drift no longer touches the glass panel's region, so Chrome stops re-blurring it every frame.

About 30 fps, 1.5× pixels

FRAME_MS 31 skips alternate frames in animated modes; the drift is slow enough that 60 is indistinguishable. Device pixel ratio is capped at 1.5 for soft glows and rings.

Painted time

Fades advance by painted frames (capped at 70 ms each). A stall pauses; it does not skip. The gap while a tab is hidden credits nothing.

Even-paced curves

A dropped frame under an even curve loses a sliver; under a fast-start curve it loses the whole opening and reads as a cut.

No shadowBlur

Glows are wide low-alpha underlay strokes and radial gradients, visually the same at a fraction of the per-frame cost. The tinted mark is cached in 64 px width buckets.

Start after paint

The canvas starts 250 ms after the page and fades in over 900 ms. Still mode renders once and stops the loop; a fresh frame is requested only on scene change, resize, motion change or returning to the tab.

will-change only while it matters

Applied to crossfading screens and ghosts for the duration of the transition, cleared in Still.

Show the rules for interrupted transitions

People click during transitions. Every rule below exists because a recording showed the pop it removes.

  • Reversal: if the next scene is the one being faded out, the same fade runs backwards from exactly where it is (fadeProgress = 1 − p; smoothstep makes both layers continue without a jump).
  • Continue from where it is: a new fade starts from the incoming layer's current alpha and current blended scale.
  • Same scene, same state: announcing the scene already on screen (a re-render that only changed copy) leaves reveals and fades exactly where they are.
  • Carry the overlay: if a whole-screen crossfade is still running when the next render arrives, the half-faded overlay is kept and finishes fading over 420 ms ease-out from its current opacity.
  • Continue the dissolve: a second click during a copy morph clones the content at its current opacity so it keeps dissolving from where it is.
  • Hover paint travels: the clone inherits the hovered or focused control's computed background, colour, transform, shadow and outline.
  • Focus lands on the live title: clones are inert, aria-hidden, stripped of ids and title markers; focus goes to .screen:not([inert]) [data-screen-title].
  • Settled stays settled: .screen-settled is added before the crossfade class is removed, so the CSS enter animation stays off afterwards.
  • The door holds still while it leaves: during the opening's exit beat the anchor glide is held; the rings travel after the mark has gone.
Show the recipe for checking motion frame by frame

Feel is measured. The transition rework was driven by frame-level analysis of screen recordings, and that is the recipe for any change to this system.

  1. Record the real product on the real display (a plain screen recording at the display's native scale, kept next to the build it came from).
  2. Score frame-to-frame change: ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "select='gte(scene,0)',metadata=print" -f null -. Two signatures to look for: a 3-beat pattern (two changed frames then a frozen one) means the main thread is over budget; single-frame pops mean a wall-clock fade ran through a stall.
  3. Check that one mark is visible at a time: in every frame, at most one of the mark and the rings is above ~0.3 alpha.
  4. Check hover continuity: the control under the cursor must not change paint at the swap frame.
  5. Check the zombie fade: about 850 ms after a home transition, opacity of the live screen must still be 1 and getAnimations() must show no screen-enter animation.
  6. In a headless or hidden pane, rAF and Web Animations clocks freeze; finish() animations manually and run rAF bodies inline. A restarted CSS animation pins at its first keyframe (opacity 0), which is how the zombie fade was caught.
  7. Then walk it in Still and with reduced transparency, and on a phone at 740 and 700 px tall.

Rules

Do and don't

Do
  • Sequence unlike marks; morph like marks at one blended scale.
  • Advance fades by painted time and cap each frame's credit.
  • Use the duration ladder: 220, 420/560, 620, 900, 1250, 2000.
  • Key transitions to content changes.
  • Start every new fade from the current alpha and scale.
  • Keep idle amplitude under 2 % with periods over 10 s.
  • Ramp every sound in and out.
  • Offer Full, Gentle, Still; let the explicit choice win.
  • Record and score frames before calling a transition done.
Don't
  • Crossfade the door mark and the rings on top of each other.
  • Fade the glass panel during in-flow clicks, or clone its blur.
  • Use fast-start easings on anything that fades.
  • Add a busy gate that drops the canvas to 20 fps; it steps the door.
  • Restart a fade when the same scene is announced again.
  • Let focus land on a transition clone.
  • Autoplay anything: video, sound, voice, guided steps.
  • Assign globalAlpha inside a drawing; multiply it, or the crossfade is discarded.
  • Add a shadowBlur, a second blur, or a full-canvas clear per frame.
