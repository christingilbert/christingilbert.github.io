Repairing a Booking Journey

Organization Himalayan Green Trips
Role Digital Experience Design · Content Strategy
Timeline 2026
Scope Information Architecture · Content Strategy · Booking Experience

Repairing the space between deciding to go and being ready to book — clearer content, and a structure a traveler can actually follow.

Overview

Himalayan Green Trips runs small-group treks and tours in Nepal, organized around responsible tourism: routes and logistics planned to leave a lighter footprint on the mountains and the people living along them. Since January 2026, I've worked with the company as an independent consultant on its website — content, information architecture, and the booking experience built on top of both.

A trip like this is a large decision made from far away, usually without ever meeting the operator in person. The site has to do the convincing a guide would normally do face to face.

The Challenge

Before this engagement, the site's content worked against the booking it was supposed to support: trip information followed the company's internal structure instead of the questions a traveler actually holds when comparing routes — cost, difficulty, season, what's included. The path to actually booking a trip was hard to find, and harder to trust once found.

A traveler who can't find the answer, or can't tell whether the operator is credible, closes the tab.

That gap costs more for a small operator than a larger one — there's no brand recognition to fall back on if the site itself doesn't do the convincing.

The Approach

The work started with the content — reading what existed against what a traveler actually needs to decide, then cutting and rewriting from there. The site's structure followed the same logic: organized around how someone chooses a trek, not around how the company is set up internally.

Booking comes last on purpose. A booking path is only as good as what's underneath it — fix the content and the structure first, and the distance between deciding to go and confirming a date gets shorter on its own.

In Progress

This is ongoing work. The content and the structure underneath it are largely in place; the booking experience is the current stage, built on top of both rather than patched onto what was there before.

There's no launch to report yet, so there's nothing to measure. What's already different: a traveler landing on the site now has less to hold in their head to work out whether this trip is right for them, and what to do next.
