Senior Experience Designer · Vancouver, BC

Making digital experiences more cognitively accessible

Hi, I'm Christin. What does a person actually need at the moment they decide, with fifteen tabs open, too many options and little attention left? My first reservation interfaces were for campground booking software in 2005. My work has grown from individual interfaces to thinking more broadly about how content and systems can make space for different ways of thinking.

View selected work

An experience designed for an overloaded mind can feel more welcoming to everyone

One of my longest-running curiosities is how people can encounter the same information yet need different things before they feel ready to commit. The same screen can invite many different ways of thinking and deciding. Some of us skim and move quickly, while others need more time to see how the pieces fit together.

Studying cognitive neuropsychology gave me a deeper understanding of the processes behind those differences, from attention and working memory to executive function. That curiosity now shapes how I think about cognitive accessibility, how I design digital experiences and how I support the people I mentor.

01My Morning Door · Chrome extension · 2026

A multi-sensory calm space for your new Chrome tab

My Morning Door turns Chrome's new tab into a quieter starting point using images I photographed. It combines ambient rain, ocean, forest and brown noise with optional voice guidance and short breathing, grounding or seated-release practices. No accounts, no analytics, and no browsing-history access.

02Nookfare · Live prototype · 2026

Choosing a stay with your sensory needs in mind

Nookfare starts with a traveller's sensory preferences, then uses them to shape how stays are presented and compared. Each stay is shown in relation to those preferences, with sensory details alongside practical information about arriving at the property.
