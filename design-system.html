Plum & Linen

The visual language for christingilbert.com. A warm near-white silk ground, deep plum for weight, and a mauve accent tuned in two steps: mauve on light, rose on dark. Every value here is verified to WCAG AA. Copy the tokens into any page to keep the site consistent.

v2.1: motion tokens (four durations, two curves, two distances) and the reduced-motion rule. v2: Plum & Linen palette (plum ink, almond-silk grounds, mauve accent shifting to rose on dark). v1.1: single-colour headings and italic quotes. v1: initial Ink & Ochre system.

Colour tokens

Two warm light tones carry the page, plum supplies the dark weight, and one mauve is the only chromatic accent. Separation comes from colour blocking, not lines.

Why two mauves? A single mid-tone mauve can't pass contrast on both a near-white ground and the plum band: too light on silk, too dark on plum. So the accent deepens to #6E4658 on light and lifts to a rose #CFA9C4 on dark. Same family, two steps. Never use rose on light or mauve on dark.

Typography

Two families. Playfair Display (400, roman and italic) sets the display scale, H1 through H4; Hanken Grotesk covers everything else: lede, body, captions, labels, buttons and data. No third face.

How to read these specs

  • The number after the font name is its weight: 400 is regular, 600 is semibold, 700 is bold.
  • rem is a text size that scales with the reader's browser font setting. 1rem is about 16px, so 1.5rem is about 24px.
  • lh is line spacing, written as a multiple of the text size. 1.65 means each line is 1.65 times the text height.
  • ls is letter spacing, the gap between characters. A small negative value tightens large headings.
  • clamp(small, ideal, large) is a flexible size: it follows the ideal, which is based on screen width, but never drops below the small value or above the large one.

Colour blocking

The page separates by fill, never by rules or dividers. Sections alternate silk and almond; one plum band anchors the middle; the footer closes on plum. Grouped items become filled cards with gaps (radius 10–12px, gap ~0.6rem). No borders.

Card fill rule: a card uses the tone its section doesn't. On a silk section, cards are almond; on an almond section, cards are silk; on the plum band, cards are violet. That one-step difference is what makes the blocking read.

Motion tokens

Motion here confirms, it doesn't perform. Things fade, rise a little or shift a few pixels; a photograph may draw a touch closer on hover. Four durations and two curves define the system for christingilbert.com; the migration notes at the end list what still has to move onto them. Nothing loops except the homepage's ambient video, and when a reader has asked their system for less motion, movement stops, the video stays still and only the fades remain.

How to read these specs

  • ms is milliseconds, thousandths of a second. 200ms is a fifth of a second: quick enough to feel immediate, long enough to be seen.
  • Easing is the speed curve of a move, how it starts and how it lands. Each curve here has a plain name and a drawn shape.
  • Reduced motion is a setting in the reader's operating system that asks sites to move less. Every pattern below has a reduced-motion version.
  • Replay runs a demo one more time. Nothing on this page loops or starts by itself.
  • View CSS opens the exact line of code behind a value, for anyone building with it. The full set is in the copy-paste block further down.

When motion stops

Your current motion preference: Full motion

When reduced motion is enabled:

  • Fades remain. Colour and opacity can still change because nothing moves across the screen.
  • Movement is removed. Content appears in place instead of rising, sliding, nudging or zooming.
  • Page navigation is immediate. Smooth scrolling becomes an instant jump.
  • Background media stays still. The homepage uses a still image instead of autoplaying video.
  • Focus indicators remain immediate. They are never animated, so keyboard users can always see where they are.

Every page carries a prefers-reduced-motion rule for its reveals and slides, and this page carries one for everything above. The demos obey it too: switch it on in your system settings and press Replay again, and the dots fade in place instead of travelling. This is the part of a motion system most sites leave out.

Why so few steps? Motion is something the reader's attention has to process, and it costs more when the reader is already carrying a lot. Four durations and two curves are enough to say "that worked", "this is arriving" and "this is changing" without asking anyone to watch. Longer moves, and anything that loops, are decisions this system doesn't make.

Migration notes

The gap between this page and the live pages, and how it closes. Same idea as the palette checklist below: the system is written down first, then the pages move onto it.

  • Photo hover runs at 400ms today. It moves to --dur-slow (300ms).
  • Reveals run at 600–750ms on the settle curve on the homepage and 800ms on the standard curve on inner pages. The migration picks one for every reveal (settle is the likelier choice: content arrives sooner and lands softly) at --dur-reveal.
  • A few homepage fades at 100ms, 900ms and 1s fold into --dur-quick and --dur-reveal.
  • Smooth scrolling and the menu icon are under the reduced-motion rule on this page; the other pages pick up the same two lines.
  • Every page then points at the tokens instead of literal values, and transition: all goes.

Copy-paste tokens

Drop this :root at the top of any page's stylesheet. The last two were hardcoded before; promote them to variables so dark sections are reusable.

:root {
  /* neutrals */
  --bg:        #FAF3EF;  /* silk, ground; light text on dark */
  --surface:   #E4DDE3;  /* almond, cards & alt sections */
  --dark:      #1A1423;  /* plum, feature band, footer */
  --ink-card:  #372549;  /* violet, cards on the dark band */
  /* text (all collapse to plum on light) */
  --headline:  #1A1423;
  --body:      #1A1423;
  --muted:     #1A1423;
  /* accent, one mauve, two steps */
  --accent:    #6E4658;  /* mauve, on LIGHT surfaces */
  --accent-h:  #523544;  /* hover on light */
  --rose:      #CFA9C4;  /* on DARK surfaces */
  /* type */
  --serif:     'Playfair Display', Georgia, serif;
  --body-fnt:  'Hanken Grotesk', system-ui, sans-serif;
  /* motion, four durations, two curves, two distances */
  --dur-quick:  150ms;  /* smallest controls */
  --dur-base:   200ms;  /* hover colour & background */
  --dur-slow:   300ms;  /* toggles, menus */
  --dur-reveal: 800ms;  /* content entering; stagger 100–150ms */
  --ease-standard: ease;                           /* gentle in and out */
  --ease-settle:   cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1); /* fast start, soft landing */
  --rise:  16px;  /* reveal travel; page headers 20px */
  --nudge: 4px;   /* hover travel for arrows */
}

Reusing this on other pages

A short checklist to bring the rest of the site onto this palette (the Ink & Ochre to Plum & Linen migration).

  • Paste the :root token block into each page's stylesheet, replacing the old Ink & Ochre variables.
  • Find-and-replace the legacy hexes: #211F1C→#1A1423, #8A5015→#6E4658, #743F11→#523544, #F2EEE5→#FAF3EF, #E5DFD2→#E4DDE3, #2E2B27→#372549, #D9A24E→#CFA9C4.
  • Watch for the old colours in rgba() form: overlays use rgba(33,31,28,…) and ochre tints use rgba(138,80,21,…), which hex find-and-replace misses. Convert to rgba(26,20,35,…) and rgba(110,70,88,…).
  • Keep line separators out: colour-block instead. Alternate silk/almond, give grouped items a contrasting fill, radius 10–12px, gap 0.6rem.
  • Use mauve --accent on light surfaces only; switch to --rose for any text on the plum band or footer.
  • Keep headings a single colour: plum on light, silk on dark. No accent inside a heading; reserve it for eyebrows, links and highlighted phrases.
  • Pair faces by role: Playfair for display, Hanken for everything else. Italics are reserved for quotes, pull-quotes and the occasional personal aside.
  • On any new dark section, set text to --bg (silk), cards to --ink-card (violet), accents to --rose, and re-check contrast.
  • Animate only opacity and transform, on the motion tokens, and wrap every move in a prefers-reduced-motion rule: fades stay, travel stops. Never transition: all.
